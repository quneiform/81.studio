CASE STUDY 01 // APPLICATION ARCHITECTURE

Building the site in Quneiform.

How Studio81 authors its public presence: one source of content, independently selectable presentation, and reversible transformations.

The Challenge: Presentation vs Canonical Truth

Web applications often tangle authored content, visual styles, and interaction state together in templates. When teams adapt copy for different audiences or experiment with themes, content easily drifts away from verified facts—leaving conflicting commitments and broken layouts.

For this site, Studio81 chose to build on Quneiform to demonstrate decoupled content and presentation. Core statements about our engineering practice (capabilities, team, and contact commitments) are immutable claims. Visual styling (q.ux.theme) and contextual wording (q.ux.content) are modeled as admitted presentations that can be chosen or reversed without altering underlying truth.

Theme-Adapted Wording in Practice

The place and era themes (Redmond 1996, SGI Indigo, Puget Twilight, Olympic Larch) explore different atmospheres and vocabulary. In Quneiform, this is handled as an optional presentation layer: an opt-in projection that adapts tone while keeping the underlying technical claims and contact endpoints exact.

Selecting a visual theme never implicitly rewrites copy. Instead, visitors can toggle theme-adapted wording below or in the theme widget to preview how the engine adapts voice across eras.
